## What this task is

Two things a teacher on a phone hit when inviting their class. First, the made
link says *Here is the link. Send it however you like* but offers only
**Copy**, so they had to copy, switch apps and paste. Now, wherever the device
has the Web Share API, the made link offers **Send** beside Copy. Send opens
the device's own share sheet with the link, and the teacher picks the class
chat. Second, right after *Put online* a song's Share led with *For people
already in the book* and *Members open Carinhoso at its address.*, and a
teacher with no students in the book yet did not read that as how to reach
them. Now, while the book has nobody but its owner, the owner's Share leads
with the invitation, and the address follows it.

## Why it needs to be done

Source: the persona exploration of 2026-10-20 (Haiku agents playing guitar
teachers drove the app with playwright-cli on a local stack; the findings were
verified live and against the code). Workflow 10 promised "sends it however
they like" (§Invitations) but gave a phone nothing to send with. And the
surface after Put online ("ready to invite", §Sharing a song or a setlist)
put the members' address, which serves nobody yet, above the invitation.
Nothing downstream depends on this task; it is part of `milestones.m5_complete`.

## Constraints / requirements

1. **Send exists only where the device can share.** `SendLinkButton` renders
   when `typeof navigator !== 'undefined' && typeof navigator.share ===
   'function'`. Where `navigator.canShare` exists, `navigator.canShare({ url
   })` must also be true. The check runs **at render**, never at module load,
   so a platform (or a test) that has the API when the link appears gets the
   button. Without it, `CreatedLink` renders exactly as today: field, Copy,
   Done.
2. **What Send passes.** Pressing Send calls `navigator.share({ url: link })`
   with the very link in the field: the book link, or the song or setlist
   link carrying `#<path>`. It passes nothing else and sends nothing itself
   (workflow 10 §Invitations: no address, no email).
3. **What Send says.**
   - The share resolves: nothing changes. The app cannot know whether
     anything was sent, so it claims nothing.
   - It rejects with `AbortError` (the person closed the sheet): nothing
     changes.
   - Any other rejection: the button's word becomes *Couldn't send*,
     announced through the same polite `est-sr` live region as Copy, and the
     link stays in the field with Copy beside it. A later press tries again.
4. **Order in the row.** Send comes before Copy, both `btn`, neither
   primary: Done stays the one primary action. `.est-sharing-linkrow` wraps:
   on a phone the field takes the row's full width and Send and Copy sit on
   the line under it; on a tablet or a computer all three fit on one line
   where the width allows.
5. **One seam for every surface.** Send lives only in `CreatedLink`, so the
   phone's song and setlist sheet, the wide panel, and the New invitation
   screen and dialog all gain it at once. The people screen's per-invitation
   *Copy link* stays as it is (Decision 4).
6. **Who leads with inviting.** A pure `leadsWithInvite({ isOwner, members })`
   in `people.js` is true exactly when `isOwner` is true and `members` is
   loaded with `members.count === 0`. It is false for a member, while
   `members` is `null` (loading), and on a load error (the caller passes
   `null`). Pending invitations do not change it: nobody has joined.
7. **The order when it leads.**
   - `HostedSongShare` (wide, song or setlist): the intro, then the owner's
     invite section (heading, body, the form or `CreatedLink`), then a rule
     and `SongAddress`, then People.
   - `HostedShareBody` summary (phone) for a song or setlist: the intro, then
     `limitHint`, then the invite button (`copy.invite`), then `SongAddress`,
     then `PeopleBar`, then the Members row.
   - `HostedShareBody` summary for a book: the intro, then `limitHint`, then
     *Invite people*, then `PeopleBar`, then the Members row, then the
     Invitations.

   Otherwise every surface keeps today's order unchanged. No element is added
   or removed, and every copy string stays. Only the order changes, so
   headings and accessible names are as before.
8. **Focus is unchanged.** The panel still focuses its heading on open
   (`SongSharePanel.jsx` L58–66), and the sheet's Dialog still takes its own
   initial focus. The reorder happens in the render after the load, before
   any input from the person, and moves no focused element.
9. **Copy**, added to all four locales:
   - `sharing.send`: 'Send' (pt-BR 'Enviar', es-419 'Enviar', it 'Invia');
   - `sharing.couldNotSend`: 'Couldn’t send' (pt-BR 'Não foi possível
     enviar', es-419 'No se pudo enviar', it 'Impossibile inviare').

   `linkReady` stays: "Send it however you like" now reads true with Send and
   without it.
10. **Accessibility.** Send is a RAC `Button` named by its word. Status is in
    words (*Couldn't send*), never colour. Keyboard: Tab reaches the field,
    then Send, then Copy, then Done. axe at WCAG 2.1 AA runs on the made link
    with and without Send, and on each reordered surface.
11. **Offline first, no book without an account.** Nothing new touches the
    network or the Worker. Making the link still needs the network, as
    today; sending one already made does not.
12. **Test fixtures** use the boards' and the example book's invented songs
    and books, never real lyrics.

## Decisions

1. **The Web Share API, feature-detected, not a share menu of our own** (no
   *WhatsApp / Email / SMS* buttons, no `mailto:` or `sms:` links). The
   device's own sheet already knows the person's apps and contacts. A list of
   our own would guess wrong, and `mailto:` moves the app toward "sending"
   the invitation, which workflow 10 rules out. Where the API is missing
   (Firefox and Chromium on Linux desktops, today), Copy is the honest
   fallback the workflow already had.
2. **`{ url }` only, no `title` or `text`.** Messaging targets put `text`
   ahead of the link, and several concatenate `title` too, so the recipient
   would get our words around the owner's message. The link alone is what
   "Send it however you like" promises, and it is what Copy puts on the
   clipboard, so the two say the same thing. Passing the book's title would
   also thread a new prop through four callers for a field most targets drop.
3. **Send says nothing on success.** `navigator.share` resolves when a target
   was picked, not when anything was sent. *Sent* would be a claim the app
   cannot check, so only a real refusal is worded. A dismissal (`AbortError`)
   is the person's own choice and needs no word.
4. **Only the made link gets Send, not the people screen's *Copy link*.** The
   note and the persona finding are about the moment a link is made. The
   people screen's Copy link (re-copying an open invitation from any device)
   is a different moment, drawn on W10 #3 and pinned by its own tests.
   `SendLinkButton` is a standalone part beside `CopyLinkButton`, so adding
   it there later is one line if the design asks. No task is registered: the
   workflow does not ask for it.
5. **"Nobody yet" means `count === 0`, decided in one pure predicate.** The
   API's count already excludes the owner (`api/openapi.yaml` L601–602).
   Pending invitations do not count: the finding was a teacher whose students
   had not joined. One predicate keeps the phone sheet and the wide panel
   from drifting apart.
6. **Reorder, not a new banner.** Moving the invitation up says "this is how
   to reach them" with the surface's own words. A *Nobody is in this book
   yet* banner would add copy and an element to every empty book, and the
   Members row already says *Nobody yet*. The address stays, below: a
   member's device of the owner's could still use it.
7. **The usual order while loading or offline.** The address needs no load
   and is useful offline, so it is never held back waiting for
   `listMembers`. The reorder lands once, on the load's answer, before the
   person acts.
8. **The wide book Share (the people screen) is unchanged.** Its head
   already carries *New invitation* above everything (W10 #3), so it already
   leads with inviting.
9. **e2e stubs `navigator.share` with `page.evaluate`, in a journey
   helper.** No Chromium build on the CI viewports exposes Web Share, and
   Playwright has no context option that emulates it, so "the platform can
   share" can only be emulated in the page. The task's note names exactly
   this ("navigator.share stubbed"). The stub replaces a platform capability
   on the live page after the real UI got there. It touches no app state,
   storage or network, which is what the spec lint guards against, and it
   does not use the banned `addInitScript`. The test also pins the real,
   unstubbed platform first (Copy alone where it cannot share). The
   alternative, launching a real browser with Web Share, does not exist for
   headless Linux runners.
10. **The journey lives in `share-needs-online.spec.js`, extending its one
    test, rather than a new test or `share.spec.js`.** Its book is exactly
    the teacher's case: just put online, nobody in it. A new test would
    repeat the sign-in and Put online (most of a minute per viewport) to
    reach the same state.
